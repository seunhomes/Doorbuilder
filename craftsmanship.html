My grandfather started this shop in his garage on Indian Grove the year I was born. He built doors because he was frustrated by what he saw being installed in the new builds going up around Bloor West Village — particle-board cores wrapped in printed laminate that warped in their first winter. He believed a door was the part of a house you touched the most, and that the part of a house you touched the most should be made of something good.

Forty-eight years later, the shop is bigger and we use better dust collection, but the rest is the same. Every door is built by one of four people. Every joint is mortise-and-tenon. Every panel floats in a groove cut by a tenoner my grandfather bought new in 1981.

On taking time

It takes us twelve weeks to make a door. Six days of that is actual labour — milling, joinery, sanding, finishing, glazing, fitting. The rest is waiting. Wood needs to acclimatise. Glue needs to set under clamps. Each of the eight coats of varnish needs forty-eight hours and a light sanding before the next.

"You can't rush a door. You can only set it down and come back tomorrow."

We charge what we charge because we are paying for time more than for materials. A walnut six-panel door is maybe $300 in hardwood at our cost. Everything else — every dollar of the rest — is hours.

On the configurator

We resisted putting one on the website for years. It felt like a betrayal of the process: a door reduced to a parts picker. But our customers were already designing their doors in their heads, on napkins, in emails with bad iPhone photos as references. The configurator just gave them somewhere to do it well, and gave us a clearer brief to start from.

It's not the door. The door comes after. The configurator is a conversation starter, dressed up to look like a transaction.

Site visit & brief

A studio principal visits your home to measure the rough opening, photograph the existing trim and adjacent rooms, and discuss intent. We mark up your configurator design with site notes.

02 Weeks 3 – 5

Stock & acclimatise

Hardwood is selected for grain and figure, rough-cut, and brought into the shop. We let it sit at our shop's relative humidity for ten days before it goes onto the saw — this is what stops doors from moving with the seasons.

StockSingle-sourced Acclimatise10 days
03 Weeks 6 – 8

Mill & assemble

Stiles, rails and panels are milled to within a 32nd. Mortises are cut with a hollow-chisel mortiser; tenons with a tenoner. The door is dry-fit, disassembled, glued with hide glue, then clamped for 48 hours.

JoineryMortise & tenon Clamp48 hours
04 Weeks 9 – 12

Finish & hang

Eight coats of catalysed conversion varnish, hand-rubbed between coats. Hardware is dry-fit at the shop, hinges mortised, then the whole assembly comes to you and our install crew hangs it in your opening.

In the shop

The four tools we
could not do this without.

Oliver No. 232

Sliding-table saw. 1953. Bought third-hand. Still the most accurate rip in the shop.

Powermatic 1791264

Hollow-chisel mortiser. Cuts square holes for tenons. Reduces a half-hour chisel job to two minutes.

Tenoner (Northfield)

My grandfather's. 1981. Cuts the tenon on the end of a rail in a single pass. We've replaced the bearings twice.

Tenax glue press

Twelve cauls. Steel screw clamps. Distributes 1,400 lbs of pressure evenly across a glued-up door.
